Find your main shutoff

  1. Go where the water line comes in. Usually the basement or crawlspace wall facing the street, or next to the water heater.
  2. Turn it clockwise until it stops. A round wheel takes several turns. A lever handle only needs a quarter turn, across the pipe.
  3. Open a faucet downstairs, then call. See a yellow Ridgeback tag on the valve? Read us the number.

Valve stuck or crumbling? Don’t force it. Call, and we’ll walk you to the shutoff at the street.

The yellow tag

Every house we work in gets a tag on its main shutoff.

In a panic, nobody remembers where the valve is. So before we leave, we make sure you can find it.

  1. Visit
    We find the main shutoff and make sure it turns.

    If it’s seized, we tell you before it becomes an emergency.

  2. Tag
    We hang a numbered yellow tag on it.

    The number links to your job record: pipe material, what we fixed, and what to keep an eye on.

  3. Next time
    Call and read us the number.

    Whoever answers can pull up your house before the truck leaves.

Older homes

Is your house still on galvanized steel pipe?

Many homes built before the 1970s were plumbed with it. It rusts from the inside, and you usually find out when a joint lets go. Here’s a two-minute check.

How to tell what your pipes are made of
Check thisWhat it means
Scratch the pipe by your water meter with a key. It’s dull silver-gray, and a magnet sticks.Galvanized steel. Plan to replace it.
