01

Burst & leaking pipes

Pipes split when they freeze, rust through, or get hit by a nail during a remodel. The first job is stopping the water. The second is finding out whether the rest of that line is about to do the same thing.

Signs

  • Water dripping through a ceiling or pooling on the floor
  • A hissing sound inside a wall with every tap closed
  • No water at all after a hard freeze

On the visit

  • Confirm the water is off, then open only what we need to
  • Cut out the damaged section and splice in new pipe
  • Pressure-test the line before we close anything up

02

No hot water

A cold shower usually has a boring cause: a pilot, a thermocouple, a heating element, or a tank full of sediment. We diagnose it first. Replacement comes up only if the tank is leaking or the repair costs more than the heater is worth.

Signs

  • Water at the base of the tank
  • Hot water runs out much faster than it used to
  • Popping or rumbling while it heats

On the visit

  • Test the burner or elements, the thermostat and the relief valve
  • Give you the repair cost and the replacement cost side by side
  • Haul the old unit away if you replace it

03

Galvanized repipes

Galvanized steel narrows inside as it rusts, so pressure drops and joints start to weep. Patching one leak usually moves the problem to the next joint. A repipe replaces the supply lines with copper or PEX, and we plan it so you’re not without water for days.

Signs

  • Brown or orange water after the taps sit overnight
  • Weak pressure on upper floors
  • Leaks at threaded joints, or rust stains on the pipe

On the visit

  • Walk the whole house and map every run
  • Written scope that lists each wall or ceiling we’d open
  • Schedule the work room by room, with water back on each night

04

Drains & sewer lines

A clog that comes back after snaking is telling you something about the pipe beyond it: roots, a sag, or a collapsed section. We run a camera down the line and show you the footage before recommending anything bigger than clearing it.

Signs

  • Several drains slow at once
  • Toilets gurgle when the washer drains
  • Water or sewage backing up at the basement floor drain

On the visit

  • Clear the line with a cable or water jet
  • Camera inspection, with the footage shown to you
  • Mark the problem spot on the ground if it needs digging

05

Hidden leaks

If your water meter moves while every tap is off, water is going somewhere. We narrow it down by isolating sections of the system and listening and measuring, so we open one small patch of wall instead of five.

Signs

  • A water bill much higher than the same month last year
  • A stain that grows, or a floor that feels soft
  • A musty smell in one room that won’t go away

On the visit

  • Meter test to confirm there’s a leak
  • Isolate branches to find which line it’s on
  • Open the smallest possible access and repair it

06

Fixtures & shutoff valves

Faucets, toilets, disposals and hose bibs. We also replace old gate valves that seize after years of never being touched, so the shutoff actually works the day you need it. Every main valve we replace gets a Ridgeback tag.

Signs

  • A shutoff that won’t turn, or drips when you try
  • A toilet that runs on its own
  • A faucet dripping at the base or the handle

On the visit

  • Swap gate valves for quarter-turn ball valves
  • Install fixtures you’ve bought, or ones we supply
  • Tag and record the main shutoff
